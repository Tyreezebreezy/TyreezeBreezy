## How I Build

**I want this thing. Can I make it?**

If yes, let's make it.

If not, can I self-host it?

Most of my projects solve an actual problem, make something in my life easier, or exist because I thought it would be cool and wanted to see if I could build it.

I'm big on things being local-first, privacy-first, customizable, and self-hosted when it makes sense.

I also care about how things feel to use. Something can technically work and still suck. I like making things that work well, look good, feel good to use, and actually do something useful.

### AI-Assisted, Not AI-Led

I use AI when I build, but I don't consider myself a vibe coder, and I'm definitely not trying to be a meat proxy for an AI.

AI helps me work outside my current skill range and turn ideas into things that actually exist. I'm still deeply involved in the process. I decide what gets built, keep the scope tight, make the decisions, review the work, test it, break it, fix it, and keep going until it does what I actually wanted it to do.

It's a tool that lets me create things I wouldn't necessarily have the skills to build completely on my own yet, while I keep getting better at programming, cybersecurity, networking, and engineering.

## Why I Self-Host

I like owning my stuff.

For me, self-hosting is mostly about privacy, ownership, and being able to make things work the way I want them to.

I don't want everything I use tied to somebody else's cloud, subscription, rules, or business decisions. Services disappear, companies change direction, features get locked behind subscriptions, and policies change.

If something matters to me and I can reasonably run it myself, I'd rather have it under my control.
